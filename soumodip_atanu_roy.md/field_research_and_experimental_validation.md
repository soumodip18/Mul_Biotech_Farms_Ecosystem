# Nasibpur Living Laboratory Outcomes (December 2019 – June 2025)

## Overview

From **December 2019 to June 2025**, Soumodip Atanu Roy independently established and operated the **Nasibpur Living Laboratory** in Hooghly, West Bengal. Spanning approximately **2,010 days (5 years and 6 months)**, the facility functioned as an experimental field research station dedicated to validating scientific concepts under real farming conditions.

Unlike a commercial production unit designed solely for profit, the Nasibpur facility operated as a long-term research and demonstration site where new technologies, cultivation systems, biological processes, and farming models were continuously tested, documented, refined, and, when necessary, discarded based on evidence.

The programme represents one of the foundational experiences that shaped the later development of Mul Biotech Farms, its technical consulting approach, digital platforms, publications, and the IRBAS™ framework.

# Scope of Field Research

During the programme, experiments were conducted across multiple agricultural and environmental domains.

## Aquaculture

Species evaluated included:

- Pangasius
- Magur
- Grass Carp
- Chital
- Pearch
- Tilapia
- Singhi
- Indian Exotic Carps
- Indian Major Carps

Research focused on stocking density, survival, feed management, seasonal performance, water quality, and production economics.

---

## Aquaponics

Field validation included:

- Tomato
- Chilli
- Basil
- Cauliflower
- Cabbage
- Strawberry 

The objective was to evaluate crop productivity, nutrient cycling, operational maintenance, and commercial feasibility under low-resource conditions.

## Agroforestry

Experimental work included:

- Banana
- Agarwood
- Mango ( Variety: Himsagar & Langra)

Observations extended beyond crop establishment to long-term growth behaviour, customer adoption, and economic viability.

## Mushrooms

Experimental work included:

- Oyster

- Cordyceps 

Observations extended beyond crop establishment to long-term growth behaviour, market feasibility and acceptance.

# Experimental Methodology

Each experiment was documented using a structured evaluation framework comprising:

- Initial investment
- Operating expenditure
- Infrastructure cost
- Number of plants or fish introduced
- Expected production
- Actual production
- Production efficiency
- Market value
- Profitability
- Technical observations
- Operational constraints

This ensured that every experiment generated usable scientific and operational evidence regardless of commercial outcome.

# Challenges Encountered

The Nasibpur Living Laboratory was developed without institutional funding, dedicated research grants, or venture capital support. As a result, experimentation was conducted under significant financial, technical, and operational constraints.

Documented challenges included:

### Financial Constraints

- Self-financed infrastructure development.
- Limited working capital.
- Continuous reinvestment of available income into new experiments.
- Research costs frequently exceeded commercial returns.

### Environmental Challenges

- Extreme seasonal temperature variation.
- Heavy monsoon conditions.
- Water quality fluctuations.
- Disease and mortality in aquaculture systems.
- Pest pressure in crop production.

### Technical Challenges

- Frequent redesign of experimental systems.
- Equipment failures.
- Low-cost infrastructure limitations.
- Iterative modification of cultivation protocols.
- Limited access to specialized laboratory facilities.

### Operational Challenges

- Managing multiple experimental systems simultaneously.
- Balancing research activities with consultancy work.
- Limited manpower.
- Manual monitoring and record keeping.
- Long experimental cycles with uncertain outcomes.

### Commercial Challenges

- Market price fluctuations.
- Variable customer demand.
- Difficulty scaling experimental systems.
- Limited commercialization of successful prototypes.

# Research Through Failure

The financial deficit of **₹144,795** should be interpreted within the context of a long-term field research programme rather than a conventional commercial enterprise.

Over more than **2,000 days**, financial resources were deliberately invested into experimentation, prototype development, infrastructure creation, documentation, and technology validation. Many experiments were intentionally exploratory, with the objective of generating knowledge rather than immediate financial return.

The programme documented both successful and unsuccessful outcomes, enabling continuous refinement of technical recommendations and reducing implementation risks in future projects.
